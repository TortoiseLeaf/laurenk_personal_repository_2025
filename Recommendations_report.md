### Executive Summary

The purpose of this report is to explore possible solutions to the problem of social media overuse and its cost to the wellbeing of the individual and the climate. The document concludes in a recommendation for how to proceed, and will explore ways in which digital wellbeing tools can be used for social media reduction and for promoting carbon literacy, to the immediate and long-term benefit of both the individual and greater environment.  

### Problem Statement

The problem is that highly addictive social media applications contribute to phone addiction and overuse, where “In 2025, 61% of smartphone addicts said social media was the primary reason they couldn’t reduce their phone usage.” (Lee. 2025). This is an issue proven to be linked to unhappiness, mental unwellness and even psychiatric disorders (Surg. A. _et al._ 2023). 
The physical environmental cost of social media use is also significant, with "the average time spent per user [on social media applications]...one user/day...is the equivalent of 1.4 km in a light vehicle."(Derudder. 2021)
The problem is therefore two-fold and intersects at the detrimental effect of Social media and phone overuse to the health of the individual, and also the greater environment. 

Specific needs around the stakeholders of the project include that the solution proposed should be un-intrusive and fit into the end-users routine with minimal effort required. Other solutions in the screen time management market have shown to require varying degrees of user parcipation, ranging from requiring the user to go outdoors and send a picture to unlock their locked apps, to repetitive activities of watching adverts to unlock their apps. These require actionable efforts where MauiScreenTime instead proposes to encourage users to spend their time away from apps of their own accord, encouraging a change in habits rather than enforcing them. 


### Description of Options

1. Social media tracker with CO2 conversions and tree planting
	A digital wellbeing tool that tracks screen time across social media applications, converts minutes spent into CO2e and compares the users CO2e usage with natural world CO2 sink events. When user screentime reduction goals are met, trees will be planted by domestic forestry initiatives. 
	By comparing CO2e usage to natural real-world events the application aims to provide alternative reading and encourage engagement with the outdoors, with the end goal of helping wean users off of highly addictive social media apps to benefit of their health and wellbeing.
	This solution will work by informing the user to create change passively through improved personal habits.
	
2. Timer manager to prevent phone overuse, visual rewards on mobile
	An application that allows the user to lock specific applications after a certain period of time to avoid overuse. For every successful adherence to the locked application (i.e. if user obeys the time restriction and does not manually unlock the application), a visual reward is granted in the Timer manager app like a flower or plant within a garden. This aims to promote a feeling of calm, contentment and ownership over the users digital habits, and also aims to encourage an emotive consideration for the natural world. This solution will work by encouraging self-care through a gamification model of taking care of a digital garden.

3. Applications blocker focussed on self-help and offers mindful exercises
	An application that blocks offending applications and offers mindfulness exercises instead, like meditations and breathing exercises. A gallery of unlockable images of nature exists in-app for when screen time reduction goals are met.
	This solves the problem by reducing screen time on problematic applications and also helps the user take care of their health with mindfulness exercises and encourages interest in the outdoors with images of nature as unlockable rewards.
	This solution will work by rewarding an improvement in the users digital habits with a wide repertoire of visual rewards.

### Evaluation Criteria & Evaluations of Each Option

Does it:
- reduce screentime
- improve user wellbeing
- encourage interest in the natural world
- improve user control over their digital habits

1. All 4 acceptance criteria met.
2. 2/4 criteria met, with an emphasis on screentime reduction and user control over habits.
3. 3/4 criteria met, with an emphasis on wellbeing and control over habits.

### Final Recommendation

After describing and evaluating the options, I will recommend the first option of the CO2e conversion and tree planting application. It informs the user of the physical weight of their digital habits and makes them real, and offers a real-world actionable reward of tree-planting linked to their efforts. This option offers a low effort, high yeild solution to the problem outlined and meets the acceptance criteria for resolving the problem. 
The added reward of contributing to an external conservation effort should incentivise and empower the user to manage their screen time, and encourage an interest in the natural world. This is a good fit for achieving the end goal of  weaning users from highly addictive social media apps while encouraging engagement with the outdoors, to benefit both the health of the user and the greater environment where "studies [have] suggested a protective effect of exposure to natural environments on mental health outcomes and cognitive function." (Jimenez. _Et al_. 2021).

### Conclusion

In conclusion the final recommendation in this report is for the CO2e tracking option with tree planting operations. It requires the minimum and most holistic participation from the user, while offering an insight into the carbon literacy around user digital habits, and offering the highest positive physical impact from all options considered in this report. Suggestions for how to implement the solution include a cross-platform mobile application, which can fit in the digital wellness tools of the device software suite. This will allow users to use the solution where it is most effective, and with the least amount deviation required from general user routines.
